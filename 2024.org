PEG is commonly used in vesicle aspiration experiments. Researchers frequently employ PEG coatings on micropipettes and other glass surfaces for several reasons:

Prevention of non-specific adhesion: PEG's ability to reduce interactions with both positively and negatively charged vesicles makes it particularly valuable in aspiration studies where maintaining vesicle mobility is critical.
Reproducibility: PEG coatings help ensure consistent experimental conditions by minimizing variability in vesicle-surface interactions, which is essential for quantitative mechanical measurements.
Compatibility with diverse lipid compositions: As you're experiencing in your work with both AOT and DOTAP vesicles, experiments often involve multiple lipid types with varying charges. PEG accommodates this diversity.
Stability: PEG coatings tend to be stable over the timeframe of typical aspiration experiments, reducing the need for recoating during experimental sessions.
Well-established protocols: The use of PEG in membrane biophysics is well-documented, with established protocols for effective coating procedures.

In the literature, you'll find PEG coatings mentioned in papers by prominent groups working on membrane mechanics, including work from labs like Evan Evans (who pioneered many aspiration techniques), David Needham, and others who have extensively studied vesicle mechanics.

Combining different molecular weight PEGs is actually a great approach for coating micropipettes with narrow tips.
Why a PEG Mixture Works Well
Using a combination of different molecular weight PEGs provides better surface coverage. The smaller PEG molecules (PEG 3300) can penetrate and coat the narrowest regions of your pipette tip, while the larger molecules (PEG 6000 and 8000) create a more robust steric barrier in the wider sections. This creates a comprehensive coating throughout the entire pipette surface.
Recommended PEG Mixture
For a 10 ml solution that will effectively coat micropipettes with 5-10 micron tips:

100 mg PEG 3300 (1% w/v) - coats the narrowest regions
50 mg PEG 6000 (0.5% w/v) - provides intermediate coverage
50 mg PEG 8000 (0.5% w/v) - creates the strongest steric barrier

This gives you a total 2% w/v PEG solution with a balanced distribution of molecular weights.

**  February  5, 2026, Thursday Pressure Calibration
Using the pressure controller for microfluidic devices by Knowteq company, we did a calibration for our pressure sensor. The zero reference varies, Y = A*x + B
where A = 0.993+-0.011
and B = 6.406+-0.015 and B is the zero reference and can vary with the height adjustment. 
In experiments it is around 8.6
And x varies from -2 to +2.5 kPa. 
Therefore, 1 Volt change leads to around 1 kPa change in pressure. 
Also, assuming 0.05 as our measure of resolution, we end up with 50 Pascal measurement resolution. 
Using this, we can estimate our volume as well.
